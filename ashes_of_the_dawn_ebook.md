# ASHES OF THE DAWN

## by Stephen

### Prologue

The Threads that bound their world were fraying. For generations, the Fajar had woven reality through the gentle pressure of Vahl, the invisible currents that flowed through all things. But now the Empire sought to control what could only be felt, to weaponize what should only be sensed.

---

## ARC 1: THE SCATTERING

## Chapter 1
# ASHES OF THE DAWN
## Chapter 1: Threadwake

Arin felt the Threads before he saw the dawn.

They shimmered at the edge of his awareness, like heat haze over summer stone. A faint pressure against his skin that wasn't wind, wasn't sun, wasn't anything most people could name. Just the Vahl, flowing through everything.

"Still trying to touch them?"

Darro's voice came from behind him, soft as morning mist. The old man sat cross-legged on the raised stone platform that served as their meeting place, his grey hair catching the first light.

"I can feel them," Arin said. His eyes remained closed. "Like trying to catch smoke."

"Threadwake." Darro's approval warmed the word. "When the Vahl notices you back. Most soldiers train for years to reach that."

Arin opened his eyes. From this vantage point, the Fajar village spread out below them like a scattering of river stones around the central fire pit. The first cookfires sent thin trails of smoke into the pale sky. The air smelled of baking bread and damp earth.

It was an ordinary morning. The last ordinary morning, though he didn't know it yet.

"The Iru-speakers say every Thread has memory," Darro continued. "Every touch leaves an echo. That's why our ancestors built this platform here. Generations of Vahl-users have sat where you're sitting."

Arin shifted. The stone felt warm beneath him, though the sun had only just cleared the eastern peaks. He could almost imagine the ghosts of those long-dead shamans breathing with him.

"Do you ever worry you'll forget them?" Arin asked. "The memories. The people."

Darro's silence stretched long enough that Arin thought he hadn't heard. Then: "Forgetting isn't the danger, boy. Remembering wrong is."

Below, the village stirred to life. Kala moved between the roundhouses, her laughter carrying on the breeze as she teased the baker's son. Seren knelt by the central fire, arranging herbs with the careful movements that meant she was praying.

"She's worried about you," Darro said, following his gaze.

"She worries about everyone."

"More about you lately."

Arin stood, brushing dust from his trousers. "I should help with the water haul."

"Stay a moment." Darro didn't move, but his voice held a weight that rooted Arin to the stone. "The Threads you feel... what do they tell you about today?"

Arin closed his eyes again, reaching for that shimmering presence. Today felt... taut. Like a bowstring drawn too tight. The Vahl hummed with an unfamiliar tension.

"Something's coming," he said.

Darro nodded. "The imperial scouts were spotted at Red Flats yesterday. They'll reach the village by midday."

Arin's eyes snapped open. "The Solarans? Here? Why?"

"They don't need reasons. They have gods and armies." Darro stood, his joints protesting. "Your Threadsense is good. Better than mine was at your age."

The compliment should have warmed him. Instead, a cold knot tightened in Arin's stomach.

"What do they want?" he asked.

"What empires always want." Darro started down the winding path toward the village. "More."

---

By midday, the tension Arin had felt in the Threads manifested as dust on the horizon.

The imperial scouts arrived not as an invading force, but as a delegation. Five soldiers in polished steel, their red cloaks bright against the muted earth tones of the village. They moved with the confident grace of Threadholders, their Vahl anchored solidly in their bodies.

The leader removed his helmet, revealing a face sharp as a blade. "I am Decurion Valerius of the Solaran Third Legion. We seek your elder."

Darro stepped forward, leaning on his staff. "I speak for the Fajar."

"Your village stands on Imperial survey lands," Valerius said without preamble. "You have three days to vacate."

A murmur rippled through the gathered villagers. Kala's hand found Arin's arm, her grip tight.

"These lands have been Fajar territory for thirty generations," Darro said. His voice remained calm, but Arin felt the old man's Vahl shift, tightening like a fist.

"The Solaran Empire recognizes no tribal claims." Valerius's eyes scanned the village, assessing. "Three days. Or we will clear the land ourselves."

One of the other soldiers dismounted, his eyes lingering on Seren where she stood by the herb garden. "Pretty thing for a savage," he murmured.

Arin felt the Threads snap tight. His own Vahl surged, a hot rush he couldn't control. The air around him thickened.

"Arin, no," Kala whispered.

But it was too late. The soldier grinned, sensing the untamed Vahl. "Threadwake. Cute."

He took a step toward Seren, his hand dropping to his sword hilt. "Maybe we should take a souvenir."

Then Darro moved.

It wasn't a step. It was a shift in the world. One moment he stood by Arin, the next he was between the soldier and Seren. The air crackled with focused Vahl.

"The half-step," someone breathed.

Darro's staff tapped the soldier's chestplate. A sound like a bell being struck echoed across the clearing. The soldier flew backward, landing hard in the dust.

Valerius's hand went to his own sword, but Darro spoke first.

"Your message is delivered. Leave our lands."

For a long moment, the only sound was the wind through the grass. Then Valerius smiled, a cold, measured expression.

"Three days," he repeated. He remounted, signaling his men. "We will be watching."

They rode away without another word, the dust settling behind them like a shroud.

Seren touched Darro's arm. "You shouldn't have done that."

"Some things can't be left unsaid," Darro replied. His eyes met Arin's. "You felt it. The moment when choices narrow."

Arin nodded, his heart pounding. The Threads still hummed with tension, but now they vibrated with something new. Not just warning. Promise.

Kala released his arm. "What happens now?"

Darro looked toward the horizon where the imperials had disappeared. "Now we remember who we are."

But Arin, watching Seren's worried face, felt the Threads whisper a different truth. Now we learn who we must become.

---

That night, Arin dreamt of fire.

Not the warm, comforting fire of the village hearth, but cold fire that consumed without heat. Imperial fire that left only ashes.

He woke sweating, the Threads screaming a warning he couldn't decipher.

Outside his roundhouse, Darro stood watching the stars. "Bad dreams?"

"The Threads won't let me sleep."

"They're restless tonight." Darro pointed toward the southern sky. "The Voidmaw constellation is bright. The elves say that means change is coming."

"What kind of change?"

"The kind that breaks things." Darro handed him a waterskin. "Seren wrote you a letter."

Arin took the sealed parchment. "Why?"

"She thinks you'll need it. When the time comes."

Arin tucked the letter into his tunic without opening it. The paper felt heavy, weighted with unspoken fears.

"Will we fight them?" he asked.

"We're Fajar. We fight when we must." Darro's eyes were ancient in the starlight. "But fighting isn't always about winning. Sometimes it's about remembering."

"Remembering what?"

"What's worth protecting."

They stood in silence as the moon rose over the Ashridge Peaks. The Threads settled into a watchful hum, waiting for dawn.

For the last dawn of the world they knew.

## Chapter 2
# ASHES OF THE DAWN
## Chapter 2: The Locked Box

The smell of fear has its own taste.

Arin realized this as he watched the village prepare. It tasted of metal and dried herbs, of sweat and the sharp tang of adrenaline. The Fajar weren't warriors by nature, but they knew how to prepare for a fight.

"They're not coming back in three days," Kala said, sorting arrowheads. Her hands moved with practiced efficiency. "They'll come tonight. Or tomorrow at dawn."

"Darro thinks they'll wait," Arin said.

"Darro believes in honor. These men have helmets instead of faces."

She wasn't wrong. Arin had felt the imperial soldiers' Vahl - cold, disciplined, devoid of the warm hum of tribal Threadsense. Like tools pretending to be people.

Seren moved through the roundhouses, placing protective charms at every doorway. Small bundles of herbs wrapped in thread, each knot tied with a whispered prayer. Her Iru-speaking was the gentlest form of Vahl-work, but today it felt like stacking pebbles against a flood.

"They're afraid of you," a voice said behind him.

Arin turned to find Lira leaning against the water trough. Her arms were crossed, her Keth-warrior stance unmistakable even in civilian clothes. She'd arrived with the dawn traders, sent by her tribe to assess the imperial threat.

"The imperials?" Arin asked.

"Your people. They watch you when you're not looking." Lira's eyes scanned the village with a soldier's assessment. "The boy who feels the Threads before they wake. Darro's favored student."

"I'm not favored. I'm available."

A ghost of a smile touched Lira's lips. "Modesty doesn't suit warriors."

"I'm not a warrior."

"You will be." She pushed away from the trough. "The Keth offer alliance. My war-chief sends twenty riders."

Arin stared at her. "Twenty? Against the Solaran Third Legion?"

"Twenty good riders are worth a hundred imperial foot soldiers. And we know the terrain." She nodded toward the eastern pass. "Your Darro's little demonstration bought us time. The imperials will scout the area before attacking. They're thorough."

"Thorough," Arin repeated. The word felt heavy.

Lira's gaze sharpened. "You feel something."

It wasn't a question. The Threads were pulling taut again, humming a warning he couldn't decipher. Not danger. Not exactly. Anticipation.

"They're already here," he said softly.

Lira didn't question him. Her hand went to the knife at her belt. "Where?"

"Watching. From the high ridges."

She followed his gaze toward the Ashridge Peaks. The mountains were still shadowed in early light, but the Threads whispered of movement in the rocks. Imperial scouts with far-seeing glasses, mapping their defenses.

"Damn their thoroughness," Lira muttered. "They'll know about the riders before they arrive."

"Will that matter?"

"It changes everything." She turned back to him, her expression grim. "Tell Darro. And find me when the fighting starts. You may be useful."

She walked away without waiting for a response, her movements economical and sure. Arin watched her go, the Threads settling back into their watchful hum.

Useful. The word echoed in his mind as he went to find Darro.

---

Darro wasn't in his roundhouse. He was at the elders' platform, staring at the locked box.

The box sat in the center of the stone circle, ancient wood bound with iron bands. No one knew what was inside. It had been there since before Darro's grandfather's time, passed down through generations of Dawn Elders with one instruction: Open only when the Dawn dies.

"Do you think it's time?" Arin asked.

Darro didn't look at him. "The Dawn hasn't died yet."

"Lira says the imperials are watching us from the ridges. They know about the Keth riders."

"Lira sees clearly. But she sees like a Keth - all tactics and terrain." Darro finally turned, his eyes weary. "The box isn't for tactics."

"What is it for?"

"Memory." Darro laid a hand on the weathered wood. "Every Fajar elder who sat here left something in this box. Not objects. Memories. Thread-echoes."

Arin stared at the box with new understanding. "It's a... library?"

"A legacy. The accumulated wisdom of our people. But once opened, the memories disperse. They can't be contained again."

"So we only get one reading."

"One chance to understand who we were before we become who we must be." Darro's hand dropped. "Not yet. The Dawn still burns."

But as Arin looked out over the village - the frightened children, the determined elders, the too-few warriors - he wondered if Darro was wrong. The Dawn wasn't burning. It was fading.

And fading things needed remembering most of all.

---

The imperial attack came at dusk.

Not the frontal assault Lira had predicted, but something worse. Silent and precise. Archers on the high ridges, picking off sentries with arrows that made no sound. Then the main force sweeping in from the west, using the setting sun to blind the defenders.

Arin found himself fighting beside Lira at the village's edge. She moved like water, her Vahl flowing smoothly from defense to attack. A true Threadweaver, her power integrated across her body in ways Arin could only dream of.

"They're testing our strength!" she shouted over the clash of steel. "Probing for weaknesses!"

Arin blocked an imperial sword with a staff, his untrained Vahl making the impact jar his bones. He was Threadwake facing Threadholders. Outmatched in every way.

Then Darro was there.

The old man moved through the battle like a ghost, his staff a blur. Where he passed, imperials fell. Not from deadly strikes, but from precise taps that disrupted their Vahl, leaving them disoriented and vulnerable.

"The half-step!" Lira breathed. "I've never seen it used in combat!"

Arin watched, mesmerized. Darro wasn't fighting. He was... correcting. Adjusting the flow of battle like a musician tuning an instrument. Each touch restored balance, however briefly.

But for every imperial Darro neutralized, two more took their place. The Solarans had numbers. And discipline.

"Fall back to the roundhouses!" Darro ordered. "Defend the children!"

The retreat became a rout. The Fajar fought bravely, but they were outnumbered and outmatched. Arin saw Kala fall, an arrow in her shoulder. Saw Seren dragged away by imperial soldiers.

"No!" he screamed, surging forward.

Lira grabbed his arm. "They want you to follow! It's a trap!"

But Arin was already moving, his Vahl responding to his rage. The Threads screamed a warning he ignored. All he saw was Seren's frightened face.

Then the world exploded.

Literally. An imperial firepot hit the elders' platform, shattering the ancient stones. The locked box flew into the air, landing near Arin's feet.

The lid had cracked open.

For a moment, everything stopped. The fighting, the shouting, even the wind seemed to hold its breath. From the cracked box poured not objects, but light. And memories.

Arin saw generations of Fajar elders staring out at this same village. Felt their hopes, their fears, their love for this patch of earth. He saw Darro as a young man, full of fire. Saw Seren's grandmother singing the dawn-song.

The memories washed over him, through him. And in their wake, he understood.

The box wasn't for reading. It was for becoming.

Darro appeared beside him, his face grim. "It's time."

He lifted the box, the remaining memories swirling around them like fireflies. Then he spoke words Arin had never heard, in a language older than the mountains.

The light intensified, wrapping around them. The imperial soldiers shielded their eyes, stumbling back.

When the light faded, Arin and Darro were gone.

The locked box lay empty on the scorched earth. The Dawn had died.

And from its ashes, something new would have to rise.

## Chapter 3
# ASHES OF THE DAWN
## Chapter 3: The Ember Road

Darkness isn't always absence. Sometimes it's memory.

Arin discovered this in the space between moments, where Darro's spell had thrown them. They stood in a corridor of shimmering energy, the walls made of light and shadow. The locked box pulsed gently in Darro's hands, its work complete.

"Where are we?" Arin asked. His voice didn't echo. It soaked into the walls like rain into dry earth.

"The Ember Road," Darro said. His face was drawn, the spell having cost him more than years. "A path between places. Between times."

Through the shimmering walls, Arin saw glimpses of their world. The Fajar village burning. Imperial soldiers herding the survivors. Lira fighting a desperate rearguard action, her Vahl a bright flame in the darkness.

"We have to go back," Arin said.

"We can't." Darro's voice was final. "The Road only goes forward. Backward would unravel us."

"Seren... Kala..."

"Are either alive or dead. The Road doesn't care which." Darro started walking, his staff tapping rhythmically against... nothing. The sound was absorbed by the glowing walls.

Arin followed, the memories from the box swirling in his mind. He saw Darro as a young man, learning the half-step from his own master. Saw generations of Fajar children playing where now only fire remained.

"The box was a fail-safe," he realized aloud. "For when everything was lost."

Darro nodded. "The elders knew this day would come. The Empire expands. Always."

"So we run?"

"We remember." Darro stopped, pointing through the wall. "Look."

The image shifted. Not their village, but a different place. A Keth war-camp in the high steppes. Lira stood before her war-chief, bloodied but unbowed.

"The Fajar are gone," she was saying. "But their knowledge survives. The boy, Arin. He carries their Threadsense."

The war-chief, a massive man named Torvin, grunted. "Threadsense won't stop imperial steel."

"No. But it might help us survive what comes next." Lira's eyes burned with cold fire. "The Empire won't stop with the Fajar. We're next."

Torvin studied her. "You want to find this boy."

"I want to unite what remains of the tribes. Before the Empire picks us off one by one."

The image faded. Darro resumed walking. "She sees the pattern. That's something."

"She thinks I'm alive."

"Are you?"

Arin had no answer. He felt... detached. The memories from the box mingled with his own until he couldn't tell where he ended and the Fajar legacy began.

They walked for what felt like hours. Through the walls, Arin watched the Empire's advance. Solaran legions sweeping across the steppes. The Urath sealing their mountain gates. The Solla negotiating surrender terms that would never be honored.

Each vision was a knife twist. His people's world ending, and he was walking away.

"Why show me this?" he asked finally. "Why not just take us to safety?"

"Safety is an illusion," Darro said. "The only real safety is understanding. And the only way to understand loss is to witness it."

He stopped again, this time before a section of wall that showed nothing but darkness. "This is where we get off."

"Where?"

"The Bind Scar. Where the world was wounded."

Darro pressed his hand against the wall. The light parted like water, revealing a rocky landscape under a bruised sky. The air smelled of ozone and old blood.

They stepped through. The Ember Road vanished behind them, leaving no trace.

Arin looked around. They stood in a canyon of black rock, the walls scarred with deep grooves. Nothing grew here. Nothing lived.

"What is this place?"

"Where the Demon Wars ended," Darro said. "Where Amon was bound."

"Amon? The Void-demon?"

"The Empire calls him a demon. The tribes know better." Darro's eyes scanned the canyon. "He was a man once. A man who thought he could master the Hollow."

Arin remembered the stories. Amon, the wizard who tried to harness the power of nothingness. Who instead became nothing.

"Why bring us here?"

"Because this is where the Fajar began." Darro pointed to a cave mouth in the canyon wall. "And where you'll find what comes next."

The cave was dark, but Arin felt Threads emanating from it. Not the warm hum of living Vahl, but something colder. Older.

"The Hollow?" he whispered.

"Echoes of it," Darro confirmed. "Amon's failure left scars in the world. Scars that never healed."

He started toward the cave. Arin hesitated, the memories from the box warning him. This place was dangerous. Not just physically. Spiritually.

"What's in there?" he asked.

"Answers," Darro said. "And better questions."

They entered the cave. The darkness swallowed them whole.

---

Inside, the world changed.

The cave walls weren't rock. They were... absence. Spaces where reality had been torn away and never properly repaired. Arin felt the Hollow's presence like a cold wind against his skin.

"Don't touch the walls," Darro warned. "The scars are still raw."

They moved deeper. The air grew colder. Arin's breath fogged. He realized he could see the Threads here without closing his eyes. They glowed faintly in the darkness, tracing patterns he'd never seen before.

"The Vahl is damaged here," he observed.

"Damaged implies it was whole once," Darro said. "The Vahl was never meant to be separated from the Hollow. They're two sides of the same thing."

"Like breath in and breath out."

Darro looked at him sharply. "Where did you hear that?"

"The box. One of the memories. An Iru-speaker talking about the nature of existence."

"Her name was Elara. My teacher." Darro's voice held unexpected warmth. "She believed the Hollow wasn't emptiness. It was potential."

They reached a chamber where the Threads converged. In the center stood a stone altar, carved with runes Arin didn't recognize.

On the altar lay a book.

It was old. Older than the locked box. Older than the Fajar. The cover was worn leather, the pages brittle with age.

"What is it?" Arin asked.

"Amon's journal. The one he kept before... before he became what he became." Darro didn't touch it. "The Empire would burn this book. The tribes would worship it. Both would be wrong."

"Why show it to me?"

"Because you're the first Fajar in generations who might understand it." Darro's eyes held a strange light. "Your Threadsense... it's different. Cleaner."

Arin stepped closer. The book seemed to pulse with its own energy. He felt the Hollow swirling around it, but also something else. A spark of... something.

Life?

He reached for the book.

"Don't," Darro said.

But Arin's fingers were already brushing the cover. The moment he touched it, the world vanished.

Not darkness. Not light. Something else entirely. A space between spaces where time had no meaning and memory was the only reality.

And in that space, a voice spoke. Not with sound, but with pure thought.

*You are not the first to seek answers here.*

Arin tried to speak, but he had no mouth. No body. Just awareness.

*The Hollow is not a weapon,* the voice continued. *It is a state of being. To master it, you must first unbecome.*

Images flooded Arin's mind. Amon as a young man, full of ambition. Amon experimenting with the Hollow. Amon losing himself to the void.

But also... something else. A flicker of light in the darkness. A possibility Amon missed.

*The Empire fears the Hollow because they cannot control it,* the voice said. *The tribes fear it because they do not understand it. You... you are different.*

Different how? Arin thought.

*You carry no ambition. Only need. And need is the key that fits many locks.*

The voice faded. The vision ended.

Arin found himself back in the cave, his hand still resting on the book. Darro was watching him, his expression unreadable.

"What did you see?" Darro asked.

"Amon. But not the demon. The man."

"And?"

"He was wrong about the Hollow. But he was also right."

Darro nodded slowly. "That's why we're here. Not to learn from his success. To learn from his failure."

Arin looked at the book, then at the cave entrance. Outside, the world was burning. His people were prisoners or corpses. And he was in a cave learning philosophy.

"We should go back," he said. "Help Lira. Help the survivors."

"We will," Darro promised. "But first, you need to understand what you're fighting for. And what you're fighting against."

He gestured to the book. "Read it. Then decide what kind of warrior you want to be."

Arin opened the cover. The first page held a single sentence, written in a hand both elegant and desperate:

"The void is not empty. It is full of everything we're afraid to become."

## Chapter 4: The Unbecoming

Reading Amon's journal was like listening to a ghost argue with itself.

Arin sat cross-legged in the cave chamber, the ancient book open on his knees. The light came from glowing moss on the walls, casting shifting patterns across the pages. Darro had retreated to the cave entrance, standing watch.

*Entry 17: The Hollow resists categorization,* Amon had written. *The Empire calls it void. The tribes call it absence. Both are wrong. It is the space between breaths. The moment before choice.*

Arin traced the elegant script with his finger. The ink had faded, but the thoughts remained sharp. Amon's journey mirrored his own in unsettling ways.

*Entry 23: Today I attempted to anchor the Hollow using Vahl-threads. The result was catastrophic. The Hollow consumed the threads, leaving behind... nothing. Not even memory of the threads' existence.*

Arin remembered Darro's warning about the cave walls. The scars where reality had been torn away. He understood now what had caused those wounds.

*Entry 31: The Imperial scholars believe power must be controlled. Our Iru-speakers believe it must be respected. Both approaches fail with the Hollow. Control requires something to control. Respect requires something to respect. The Hollow is neither.*

A sound from the cave entrance drew Arin's attention. Darro was speaking with someone. A woman's voice, familiar but strained.

He rose, tucking the journal into his belt. The Threads hummed a warning mixed with... recognition?

At the cave mouth, Darro stood facing Lira. She was bloodied, her Keth leathers torn. An angry burn marked her cheek. But her eyes burned brighter than any fire.

"How did you find us?" Darro asked, his voice tight.

"The Threads led me," Lira said. Her gaze shifted past Darro to Arin. "You're alive."

"You're hurt," Arin said.

"The imperials have hunting parties sweeping the Bind Scar. They know you escaped."

Darro grunted. "Valerius is thorough."

"Valerius is dead," Lira said bluntly. "Crassus commands the Third Legion now. He's not interested in prisoners."

Arin felt a cold chill. Crassus. The name even Darro spoke with respect. The Empire's finest strategist. A man who considered mercy a tactical error.

"How many survived?" Arin asked.

"Of the Fajar? A dozen. Hidden in the high caves. Seren leads them."

Seren. Alive. The relief was so sharp it hurt.

"And the Keth?" Darro asked.

"We lost twenty riders. But we hurt them. Torvin approved the alliance."

"Alliance?" Arin said.

Lira's eyes narrowed. "The tribes must unite or die separately. The Fajar may be scattered, but their knowledge survives. With you."

Darro sighed. "The boy isn't ready."

"The Empire won't wait for him to be ready." Lira gestured toward the canyon. "Crassus has mages scrying the area. They'll find this cave soon."

Arin touched the journal at his belt. "I've been reading Amon's work."

Lira's eyes widened. "Amon's journal? Here?"

"You know it?"

"Every shaman knows the legend. The book that drove a man mad with power."

"Or wisdom," Darro countered. "Amon wasn't mad. He was... ahead of his time."

Lira shook her head. "We don't have time for philosophical debates. We need to move. Now."

As if on cue, a horn sounded from the canyon below. Imperial. Close.

"They're here," Darro said.

Lira drew her knife. "I'll draw them off. You two head for the high caves. Seren will be waiting."

"No," Arin said. The word surprised him as much as them. "I'm not running again."

Darro and Lira both stared at him.

"What did you say?" Darro asked.

"I'm tired of watching my people die while I learn lessons." Arin's hand tightened on the journal. "Amon wrote that the Hollow requires unbecoming. Maybe that's what I need to do."

"Unbecome what?" Lira asked.

"Afraid."

Another horn, closer this time. Shouts echoed through the canyon. The imperials had found their trail.

Darro studied Arin's face. "What are you planning, boy?"

"Amon tried to control the Hollow. That was his mistake." Arin's voice grew steadier. "He should have invited it in."

Lira grabbed his arm. "That's suicide. The Hollow consumes everything."

"Not everything," Arin said. "It can't consume what isn't there."

He closed his eyes, reaching for the Threads. But instead of grasping them, he pushed. Not away. Deeper.

Past the Threads. Past the Vahl. Into the space between.

The Hollow.

It was like stepping into winter water. Shockingly cold. Then... nothing. No sensation. No self. Just awareness.

*You understand,* a voice said in his mind. Not Amon's. Something older.

*I think so,* Arin thought back.

*Then show me.*

Arin opened his eyes. The world had changed. The cave walls were translucent. He could see the imperial soldiers approaching - five Threadholders moving with disciplined precision.

But he could also see their Vahl. Bright threads anchored in their bodies. And around those threads... spaces. Gaps where the Hollow already existed.

"Arin?" Lira's voice sounded distant.

He stepped past her, toward the cave entrance. The soldiers spotted him, raising their swords.

"There!" their leader shouted.

Arin didn't raise his hands. Didn't summon Vahl. He simply... invited.

The Hollow responded.

It flowed through him, not as power but as absence. Where it touched the soldiers' Vahl, their power simply... ceased. Not destroyed. Unmade.

The lead soldier stumbled, his sword suddenly heavy in his hand. "What did you do?"

"Nothing," Arin said. And it was true.

The Hollow wasn't a weapon. It was a state. By entering it fully, he'd created a zone where Vahl couldn't exist.

The soldiers backed away, confusion on their faces. Their training hadn't prepared them for this. For nothing.

"Fall back!" their leader ordered. "Report to Crassus!"

They retreated quickly, their discipline overcoming their fear.

Arin let the Hollow recede. The world snapped back into focus. The cave walls became solid again. The Threads hummed their familiar song.

He staggered, suddenly exhausted. Lira caught him.

"What was that?" she whispered.

"The first step," Darro answered. His eyes held a mixture of awe and fear. "The unbecoming."

Arin looked at his hands. They looked the same. But he felt different. Lighter. As if part of him had stayed in the Hollow.

"We should go," he said. "Before Crassus sends more."

Lira nodded slowly, her gaze still fixed on him. "The high caves are three hours' climb. Can you make it?"

"I have to."

Darro retrieved the journal from where it had fallen. "Amon would be fascinated. And terrified."

"Why terrified?" Arin asked.

"Because you're doing what he never dared. You're not trying to master the Hollow. You're learning to live with it."

They left the cave, the Bind Scar spreading before them like a wound in the earth. Behind them, Amon's journal lay forgotten on the stone floor.

But its words remained in Arin's mind, echoing with new meaning.

*The void is not empty. It is full of everything we're afraid to become.*

And for the first time, Arin wasn't afraid.

"What kind of cost?"

"It strips away my Vahl," Arin explained. "Leaves me hollow."

Lira leaned forward. "But you could scout their positions. Get closer than any of us could."

"And if they catch him?" Darro asked.

"They won't see him," Lira said. "That's the point."

Torvin considered this. "We need to know their numbers. Their positions. Whether Crassus is with them."

The name sent a chill through the tent. Crassus. The Empire's finest strategist. The man who believed conquest was Solar's will.

"I'll do it," Arin said. The words surprised him as much as everyone else.

Darro touched his arm. "Boy..."

"They need to know," Arin said. "And I'm the only one who can find out."

Lira nodded approval. "I'll go with him. As far as I can."

"No," Torvin decided. "He goes alone. Less chance of detection."

Darro's protest died unspoken. The Keth way was clear: risk one to save many.

---

An hour later, Arin stood at the edge of the war-camp, looking east toward the imperial positions. The land stretched away in rolling hills, dotted with thorny bushes and stunted trees. Good ground for ambushes. Bad ground for survival.

"Remember," Darro said, his voice low. "The Hollow isn't a cloak you wear. It's a state you inhabit."

"I know," Arin said.

"Don't stay too long. And don't try to control it. Let it guide you."

Lira handed him a waterskin. "If you see Crassus... don't do anything stupid."

"Like what?"

"Like thinking you can take him alone," she said. "He's Threadmeld. You're... whatever you are."

Threadwake facing Threadmeld. It was like a candle facing a bonfire.

Arin nodded, then turned and started east. The land felt different outside the camp's protective energy. More exposed. More dangerous.

He walked for an hour, his senses stretched taut. The Threads here were different too - less the warm hum of tribal Vahl, more the cold discipline of imperial presence.

When he felt the first imperial scout's Vahl, he stopped. It was a careful distance away, watching the war-camp from concealment. Arin could feel the scout's focused attention, the disciplined stillness of trained observation.

Time for the Hollow.

He didn't reach for it. Instead, he let go. Of his fear. His determination. Even his sense of self.

The world faded. Colors muted. Sounds became distant echoes. He was still there, but... less.

He continued walking, passing within twenty paces of the imperial scout. The man never reacted. His Vahl remained focused on the war-camp, completely unaware of Arin's presence.

The Hollow worked.

He moved deeper into imperial territory, passing more scouts. None detected him. They were like statues, their Vahl anchored in disciplined observation.

Then he felt it.

A different presence. Older. More powerful. Not the sharp focus of scouts, but the calm authority of command.

Crassus.

Arin moved toward the presence cautiously. Through the Hollow's filtering effect, he saw a command post set up in a shallow dell. Imperial officers moved with efficient precision around a central figure.

Crassus stood taller than the others, his imperial armor polished to a mirror shine. His Vahl wasn't just anchored - it was woven through his entire being, a seamless integration that spoke of Threadmeld mastery.

But it was the man's presence that struck Arin most. Not aggression. Not hostility. A calm, absolute certainty. Like a mountain that knows it cannot be moved.

Crassus was studying a map, his gestures precise. Arin moved closer, the Hollow making him invisible to Vahl-sense.

"...the Keth have Fajar survivors," one officer was reporting. "Including the Threadseer boy."

Crassus didn't look up from his map. "The boy is interesting. But not important. The tribes must learn they cannot hide from Solar's light."

"Do we attack the war-camp?"

"Not yet," Crassus said. "Let them fear first. Fear is a better weapon than steel."

Arin felt a cold dread settle in his stomach. This wasn't a man driven by rage or ambition. This was something worse. A man who believed he was doing holy work.

Crassus finally looked up, his gaze sweeping the area. For a terrifying moment, Arin thought the commander could see him. Those calm eyes seemed to look right through the Hollow.

But then Crassus returned to his map. "Prepare the bombardment teams. We strike at dawn."

Bombardment. The word echoed in Arin's mind. They weren't planning a ground assault. They were going to shell the war-camp from a distance.

He had to warn them.

Arin backed away carefully, maintaining the Hollow state. But as he retreated, something changed. Crassus's head snapped up again, his eyes narrowing.

"Something is here," the commander said softly.

Arin froze. Could Crassus sense the Hollow?

One of the officers shook his head. "The area is clear, sir."

"Not clear," Crassus said. "Empty."

His eyes scanned the dell, and for the second time, they seemed to look right at Arin. Not through him. At him.

"Interesting," Crassus murmured. "Very interesting."

Arin didn't wait. He turned and ran, letting the Hollow drop as he put distance between himself and the command post. The world snapped back into focus, colors bright, sounds sharp.

He ran back toward the war-camp, his mind racing. Crassus had sensed him. The Hollow wasn't as undetectable as they'd thought.

And worse, the imperials were planning to shell the camp at dawn.

They had hours. Maybe less.

The testing was over. The war was about to begin.
