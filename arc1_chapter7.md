# ASHES OF THE DAWN
## Chapter 7: Dawn Departure

Dawn in the high caves arrived without fanfare. The light crept slowly down the cave walls, turning the darkness from black to grey to amber. Arin had barely slept, his mind racing with possibilities and fears.

Seren was already awake, moving among the sleeping forms with quiet efficiency. She checked bandages, distributed food, whispered reassurances. Her calm was a bedrock in the shifting uncertainty.

"They're scared," she said when she reached Arin. "But they'll follow you."

"Me?" Arin said, surprised. "I'm no leader."

"You're Fajar," she said simply. "And you carry Amon's journal."

Arin touched the book at his belt. It felt heavier than it had yesterday. "I barely understand it."

"Understanding will come," Seren said. "When you need it."

The Fajar began to stir. Children whimpered, awakened by the cold. Adults rose stiffly, their movements slow with grief and exhaustion. Kala moved among them, her usual laughter silenced but her hands steady.

Darro stood at the cave entrance, watching the sky lighten. "The imperials will send scouts at first light."

Lira joined him, her gear already packed. "We should leave within the hour."

"How many can travel?" Darro asked.

"Twenty," Seren said. "The rest will stay with me."

Arin looked at the wounded. An old woman with a bandaged leg. A boy with burns across his back. Seren herself, though she pretended otherwise.

"You're not coming?" he asked her.

"Someone has to care for those who can't," she said.

"But the imperials..."

"Will find an empty cave," Lira finished. "We'll make sure of that."

Darro grunted agreement. "We'll leave signs pointing north. Toward the Urath mountains. That should buy you time."

Seren touched Arin's arm. "Be careful. The Keth are allies of necessity, not friendship."

"I know."

The Fajar gathered what little they could carry. Food. Water. Their few remaining weapons. The mood was somber, but resolve showed in their eyes. These were people who had already lost everything. They had nothing left to fear.

Kala approached Arin, her hands full of herbs. "For the journey. They'll help with the fatigue."

Arin took the small bundle. "Thank you."

"Take care of the old man," she said, nodding toward Darro. "He pretends to be strong, but the years weigh heavy."

"I'll try."

The departure was quiet. No speeches. No farewells. Just a steady stream of Fajar filing out of the cave, their faces set against the morning light.

Arin was among the last to leave. He paused at the cave mouth, looking back at Seren.

"Will I see you again?" he asked.

Seren smiled, a small, sad expression. "The Threads bind us all, Arin. We're never truly separate."

She handed him a small, folded paper. "A letter. For when you need it."

It was the same letter she'd given him before the attack. The one he'd tucked away and forgotten.

"Why now?" he asked.

"Because you're ready," she said. "Or you will be."

He tucked the letter into his tunic, next to Amon's journal. Then he turned and followed the others into the dawn.

The path led down from the high caves, winding through rocky terrain dotted with stubborn shrubs. The Fajar moved with practiced silence, their footsteps barely audible on the stone.

Lira walked at the front, her eyes constantly scanning the horizon. Darro stayed toward the rear, his staff tapping rhythmically against the rock.

Arin walked in the middle, surrounded by his people. Their quiet determination was a shield against his doubts.

After an hour, Lira signaled a halt. They'd reached the edge of Fajar territory, marked by a ring of ancient standing stones.

"From here, we're in contested lands," she said. "Imperial patrols range this far."

Darro examined the stones. "The old border markers. They've stood since before the Empire existed."

"They won't stand much longer if Crassus has his way," Lira said.

Arin touched one of the stones. It was cool beneath his fingers, its surface worn smooth by centuries of wind and rain. He could feel the faint echo of Vahl within it. Generations of Fajar had touched this stone, leaving their mark.

"Do you feel that?" he asked Darro.

The old man nodded. "The stones remember. That's why the Empire fears them."

"Why?"

"Because memory is resistance," Darro said. "And the Empire cannot tolerate resistance."

Lira made an impatient sound. "We should keep moving. Sentiment won't stop imperial steel."

But as they moved past the standing stones, Arin felt a shift in the Threads. The familiar Fajar resonance faded, replaced by something harsher. More aggressive.

Keth territory.

It smelled different too. Less of herbs and earth, more of horse and leather. The land opened up into rolling hills covered in tough, spiky grass.

In the distance, Arin saw movement. Riders. Keth scouts, their red cloaks bright against the green.

"They know we're coming," Lira said. "Good. That means Torvin is waiting."

"What if he's not?" Arin asked.

"Then we die here," Lira said matter-of-factly. "But my people honor their word. Mostly."

The riders approached at a canter, their movements fluid and confident. They reined in a respectful distance away, their leader raising a hand in greeting.

"Lira," he said. "You bring guests."

"Survivors," Lira corrected. "The Empire took their home."

The Keth leader's eyes swept over the Fajar. "They look like refugees."

"They're warriors," Darro said, his voice cutting through the tension. "Who have faced imperial steel and lived."

The Keth leader studied Darro, then nodded slowly. "Torvin awaits at the war-camp. Follow us."

He turned his horse, signaling his riders to form an escort around the Fajar. Arin felt the weight of their stares. The unspoken judgment.

Refugees. Warriors. Survivors.

He wasn't sure which they were. Or which they needed to be.

As they followed the Keth riders toward the horizon, Arin glanced back at the standing stones. They stood silent against the sky, markers of a world that was vanishing.

And ahead, the uncertain promise of a world that might yet be born.
