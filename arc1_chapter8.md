# ASHES OF THE DAWN
## Chapter 8: The War-Camp

The Keth war-camp sprawled across a high valley, a temporary city of leather tents and smoking cookfires. It smelled of horse, sweat, and the sharp tang of forge-smoke. To Arin's Threadsense, it felt like a hornet's nest - buzzing with aggressive energy.

The Keth riders led them through the camp's perimeter, past watchful sentries and curious warriors. Arin saw faces hardened by conflict, eyes that measured threat in every newcomer. These were not farmers turned soldiers. These were warriors born and bred.

Torvin awaited them in the camp's center, standing before a large command tent. He was a mountain of a man, his arms thick with muscle, his face scarred from countless battles. His Vahl was a solid anchor point in the camp's chaotic energy.

"Lira," he greeted, his voice a rumble like distant thunder. "You bring fewer than promised."

"The Empire was thorough," Lira said, her voice tight.

Torvin's gaze swept over the Fajar survivors. "They look beaten."

Darro stepped forward, his staff tapping the ground. "We are survivors. There is a difference."

"Is there?" Torvin's eyes narrowed. "Survivors run. Warriors stand."

"We stood," Arin said, the words coming before he could stop them. "And we lost. But we're still here."

All eyes turned to him. Torvin studied him with new interest. "You're the Threadseer? The one Lira spoke of?"

"I have Threadsense," Arin confirmed.

"Show me."

The challenge hung in the air. Arin felt the Fajar tense around him. Darro's warning look. Lira's subtle shake of her head.

But Arin remembered the Hollow. The cold certainty of that space.

He closed his eyes, reaching not for the Threads, but past them. Into the emptiness.

For a moment, nothing happened. Then Torvin gasped.

"What did you do?" the war-chief demanded.

Arin opened his eyes. The world had shifted slightly. Colors were muted. Sounds distant. "I invited the Hollow in."

Torvin took a step back, his hand going to his sword. "That's... unnatural."

"It's power," Lira said quickly. "Power the Empire can't touch."

"Power that touches back," Darro warned.

Arin let the Hollow recede. The world snapped back into focus. "It's a tool. Like your sword."

Torvin stared at him, his expression unreadable. "A sword doesn't unmade the world around it."

"Neither does the Hollow," Arin said. "It just... makes space."

The war-chief considered this. "Space for what?"

"For us," Arin said. "To be something the Empire doesn't expect."

Torvin grunted, then turned to Lira. "Your pet shaman has spirit. I'll give him that."

"He's not my pet," Lira said sharply.

"No," Torvin agreed. "But he's something. That much is clear."

He gestured to one of his warriors. "Find them tents. Food. They look like they haven't eaten in days."

As they were led away, Arin felt the weight of staring eyes. The Keth watched them with a mixture of curiosity and suspicion. Refugees from a fallen tribe, carrying strange powers.

Darro walked beside him, his voice low. "That was foolish, boy. Showing that power so openly."

"They needed to see," Arin said.

"They saw," Darro agreed. "But what they'll do with what they saw..." He left the thought unfinished.

Their tents were small but serviceable, set apart from the main Keth areas. The Fajar settled in quietly, their exhaustion finally overwhelming them.

Kala distributed the food the Keth had provided - tough journey-bread, dried meat, water from a nearby stream. It wasn't much, but it was more than they'd had in days.

As darkness fell, the war-camp transformed. Cookfires blossomed like orange flowers in the night. The sound of laughter and song drifted through the air, mingling with the ever-present smell of smoke.

Arin sat outside his tent, watching the Keth prepare for whatever came next. They moved with purpose, their movements economical and practiced. Every action served survival.

Lira joined him, her own meal in hand. "They're not so different from us," she said, following his gaze.

"They're louder," Arin observed.

"Warriors are always louder than shamans," Lira said. "Less to think about."

"Is that what you think of us? That we think too much?"

Lira chewed thoughtfully. "I think you see things we don't. And miss things we see clearly."

"Like what?"

"Like the fact that Torvin is testing you," she said. "That display with the Hollow... he'll want to see more."

"What kind of more?"

Lira's expression was grim. "The kind that gets people killed."

Arin looked at the Keth warriors practicing their drills in the firelight. Their movements were brutal, efficient. No wasted motion. No hesitation.

"Could we learn to fight like that?" he asked.

"You could," Lira said. "But should you? That's the question."

Darro emerged from his tent, his face illuminated by the campfires. "The Threads are restless tonight."

Arin felt it too. A tension in the air that had nothing to do with the Keth. Something bigger was stirring.

"Imperial movements?" Lira asked.

"Maybe," Darro said. "Or something else."

He pointed toward the southern horizon. "The Bind Scar lies that way. Where we found Amon's journal."

"What about it?" Arin asked.

"The scars are healing," Darro said. "Or... changing."

Arin remembered the cave walls where reality had been torn. The spaces where the Hollow lingered.

"Could the Hollow be spreading?" he asked.

Darro's silence was answer enough.

In the distance, the Keth war-drums began to beat. A steady, ominous rhythm that echoed through the valley.

The testing, it seemed, was about to begin.
