## Cycle 4 Task: ACTIVE
**Investigation:** Track identity preservation through occlusion and density limits

**Key Challenge from Cycle 3:**
Structured illumination encoding (r01) enables single-frame depth estimation, but particles may occlude each other in dense configurations. Temporal tracking (r02) must resolve "which particle appeared where" when 2+ particles align in same camera view.

**Critical Questions:**
1. At what particle density does occlusion degrade tracking performance below 95% identity preservation?
2. Can multi-camera geometry (r01 output) resolve occlusions computationally?
3. What is the worst-case occlusion scenario and can r03 handle it gracefully (particle dropout acceptable)?

**Mathematical Framework:**
1. Ray-cast occlusion model: Given N particles in volume V, P(occlusion) vs camera angle diversity
2. Information recovery: Can remaining cameras reconstruct occluded particle position?
3. Track fragmentation rate: When particles merge/split in projection, can temporal coherence recover identity?

**Algorithm Development:**
- Sparse scene (10-50 particles): Full tracking via temporal coherence
- Dense scene (500+ particles): Probabilistic tracking, identity uncertainty accepted
- Occlusion handling: Multi-hypothesis tracking (GPU-expensive? r03 concern)

**Deliverable:** 
1. Particle density function: trackable(N, V, camera_count) with 95% confidence
2. Compute complexity estimate: O(N^k) scaling for k=1,2,3 (r03 needs this)
3. Hardware architecture recommendation: GPU vs FPGA detection pipeline
