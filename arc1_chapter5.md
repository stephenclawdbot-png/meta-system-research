# ASHES OF THE DAWN
## Chapter 5: The High Caves

Exhaustion wasn't the right word. 

It was deeper than that. More like parts of Arin had been scoured away, leaving hollow spaces where his Vahl used to be. The walk to the high caves lasted three hours, but it felt like three days. Each step was a conscious effort, his feet dragging through the rocky soil.

Lira watched him sideways, her expression unreadable. Darro walked ahead, his back straight despite the climb. The old man seemed to draw strength from the mountain, his steps sure even on the steepest paths.

"How far?" Arin managed. His voice sounded thin, stripped of resonance.

"Not far," Lira said. "The entrance is hidden behind a waterfall."

"Waterfall? In this drought?"

"The mountain gives when the world takes."

Arin didn't have the energy to argue. The Hollow still lingered in his senses, a cold echo that muted the Threads' usual hum. He missed their warmth, their familiar pressure against his skin.

Darro stopped abruptly, holding up a hand. "Voices ahead."

Arin strained to listen. Over the wind, he caught faint sounds. Not Imperial discipline. The loose rhythm of tribal speech.

"Fajar," Darro confirmed. "Seren's voice among them."

Relief washed through Arin, so sharp it hurt. Seren was alive. The knowledge anchored him in a way even the Threads hadn't.

They rounded a bend and saw the waterfall Darro had mentioned. It wasn't the roaring cascade of legend, but a thin veil of water trickling down black rock. Behind it, a cave mouth yawned into darkness.

"This way," Lira said, ducking through the water.

Inside, the air changed. It was cooler, scented with herbs and smoke. Torches burned in sconces along the walls, their light catching on faces Arin recognized.

Kala sat by the central fire, her hands busy with weaving. The baker's son knelt beside her, grinding herbs. Other Fajar survivors moved through the chamber with the quiet efficiency of people who'd outrun death.

And then there was Seren.

She stood near the back of the cave, directing two women carrying water skins. When she saw them, her hands stilled. Her eyes met Arin's across the chamber.

For a long moment, nobody moved. Then Seren crossed the space between them and wrapped Arin in a tight embrace.

"You're alive," she whispered into his shoulder. Her voice cracked.

"You too," he said, his own throat tight.

When she pulled back, her eyes were dry but bright. "We lost so many."

"I know."

Darro cleared his throat. "How many survived?"

"Twenty-three," Seren said. "Mostly women and children. The imperials took the warriors who surrendered."

"Surrendered?" Arin repeated, stunned.

"Some chose chains over death," Seren said quietly. "Can we blame them?"

Lira made a disgusted sound. "Chains are just slow death."

"Maybe," Seren allowed. "But it gives them time. Hope."

Arin looked around the cave. It was larger than he'd first thought, with tunneled passages leading deeper into the mountain. The Fajar had made it a home, however temporary. Bedrolls lined the walls. A cooking area smoked near one entrance. Children played quietly in a corner.

It was familiar and alien all at once. Their people's way of life, crammed into a hole in the mountain.

"The imperials will find this place," Darro said. "It's only a matter of time."

Seren nodded. "We know. That's why we're not staying."

"Where will you go?"

"The Keth have offered sanctuary," Lira said. "Their war-camp in the steppe valleys. Defensible ground. Fresh water."

Arin looked between them. "The Keth? After what happened at the trade summit?"

Lira's jaw tightened. "Times change. The Empire makes allies of enemies."

"And what do they want in return?" Darro asked.

"Our knowledge," Seren said. "Our Threadsense. They have warriors. We have shamans. Together..."

"Together we might survive," Lira finished.

Arin thought of Amon's journal, still tucked in his belt. Of the Hollow that had unmade the imperial soldiers. "There might be another way."

All eyes turned to him.

"What did you do back there?" Lira asked. "With the imperials."

"I invited the Hollow in," Arin said. "And it... helped."

Darro shook his head. "The Hollow isn't a tool, boy. It's a state. A dangerous one."

"Dangerous for who? The imperials couldn't touch me."

"Because you were touching nothing," Darro snapped. "You can't live in that state. Amon tried. Look what happened to him."

"Amon became a demon," Seren said, her eyes wide.

"Amon became what the Empire feared most," Darro corrected. "Someone they couldn't control."

Lira studied Arin with new interest. "You really did that? Stood against Threadholders with empty hands?"

"They weren't empty," Arin said. "They were... different."

"Show me," she challenged.

"Not here," Darro warned. "Not around the children."

But Arin saw the curiosity in their faces. The desperate hope. They'd lost everything. Now they were being asked to trust ancient enemies. To put their faith in alliances built on desperation.

"Maybe Darro's right," he said finally. "Maybe the Hollow isn't the answer."

But as he said it, he felt the cold echo in his bones. The part of him that had stayed in that nothing-space. It whispered that Darro was wrong. That the old ways had failed. That something new was needed.

Seren touched his arm. "You look exhausted. Rest. We'll talk more tomorrow."

She led him to a bedroll near the fire. The warmth felt good after the Hollow's chill. But as Arin lay down, he realized the warmth wasn't just from the fire.

The Threads were returning. Slowly. Faintly. Like embers stirring after a long cold night.

He closed his eyes, reaching for them. But instead of their familiar hum, he felt only the Hollow's lingering silence.

And in that silence, a new thought formed.

Maybe surviving wasn't enough. Maybe they needed to become something the Empire couldn't imagine.

Something new.
