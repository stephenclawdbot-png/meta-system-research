# ASHES OF THE DAWN
## Chapter 2: The Locked Box

The smell of fear has its own taste.

Arin realized this as he watched the village prepare. It tasted of metal and dried herbs, of sweat and the sharp tang of adrenaline. The Fajar weren't warriors by nature, but they knew how to prepare for a fight.

"They're not coming back in three days," Kala said, sorting arrowheads. Her hands moved with practiced efficiency. "They'll come tonight. Or tomorrow at dawn."

"Darro thinks they'll wait," Arin said.

"Darro believes in honor. These men have helmets instead of faces."

She wasn't wrong. Arin had felt the imperial soldiers' Vahl - cold, disciplined, devoid of the warm hum of tribal Threadsense. Like tools pretending to be people.

Seren moved through the roundhouses, placing protective charms at every doorway. Small bundles of herbs wrapped in thread, each knot tied with a whispered prayer. Her Iru-speaking was the gentlest form of Vahl-work, but today it felt like stacking pebbles against a flood.

"They're afraid of you," a voice said behind him.

Arin turned to find Lira leaning against the water trough. Her arms were crossed, her Keth-warrior stance unmistakable even in civilian clothes. She'd arrived with the dawn traders, sent by her tribe to assess the imperial threat.

"The imperials?" Arin asked.

"Your people. They watch you when you're not looking." Lira's eyes scanned the village with a soldier's assessment. "The boy who feels the Threads before they wake. Darro's favored student."

"I'm not favored. I'm available."

A ghost of a smile touched Lira's lips. "Modesty doesn't suit warriors."

"I'm not a warrior."

"You will be." She pushed away from the trough. "The Keth offer alliance. My war-chief sends twenty riders."

Arin stared at her. "Twenty? Against the Solaran Third Legion?"

"Twenty good riders are worth a hundred imperial foot soldiers. And we know the terrain." She nodded toward the eastern pass. "Your Darro's little demonstration bought us time. The imperials will scout the area before attacking. They're thorough."

"Thorough," Arin repeated. The word felt heavy.

Lira's gaze sharpened. "You feel something."

It wasn't a question. The Threads were pulling taut again, humming a warning he couldn't decipher. Not danger. Not exactly. Anticipation.

"They're already here," he said softly.

Lira didn't question him. Her hand went to the knife at her belt. "Where?"

"Watching. From the high ridges."

She followed his gaze toward the Ashridge Peaks. The mountains were still shadowed in early light, but the Threads whispered of movement in the rocks. Imperial scouts with far-seeing glasses, mapping their defenses.

"Damn their thoroughness," Lira muttered. "They'll know about the riders before they arrive."

"Will that matter?"

"It changes everything." She turned back to him, her expression grim. "Tell Darro. And find me when the fighting starts. You may be useful."

She walked away without waiting for a response, her movements economical and sure. Arin watched her go, the Threads settling back into their watchful hum.

Useful. The word echoed in his mind as he went to find Darro.

---

Darro wasn't in his roundhouse. He was at the elders' platform, staring at the locked box.

The box sat in the center of the stone circle, ancient wood bound with iron bands. No one knew what was inside. It had been there since before Darro's grandfather's time, passed down through generations of Dawn Elders with one instruction: Open only when the Dawn dies.

"Do you think it's time?" Arin asked.

Darro didn't look at him. "The Dawn hasn't died yet."

"Lira says the imperials are watching us from the ridges. They know about the Keth riders."

"Lira sees clearly. But she sees like a Keth - all tactics and terrain." Darro finally turned, his eyes weary. "The box isn't for tactics."

"What is it for?"

"Memory." Darro laid a hand on the weathered wood. "Every Fajar elder who sat here left something in this box. Not objects. Memories. Thread-echoes."

Arin stared at the box with new understanding. "It's a... library?"

"A legacy. The accumulated wisdom of our people. But once opened, the memories disperse. They can't be contained again."

"So we only get one reading."

"One chance to understand who we were before we become who we must be." Darro's hand dropped. "Not yet. The Dawn still burns."

But as Arin looked out over the village - the frightened children, the determined elders, the too-few warriors - he wondered if Darro was wrong. The Dawn wasn't burning. It was fading.

And fading things needed remembering most of all.

---

The imperial attack came at dusk.

Not the frontal assault Lira had predicted, but something worse. Silent and precise. Archers on the high ridges, picking off sentries with arrows that made no sound. Then the main force sweeping in from the west, using the setting sun to blind the defenders.

Arin found himself fighting beside Lira at the village's edge. She moved like water, her Vahl flowing smoothly from defense to attack. A true Threadweaver, her power integrated across her body in ways Arin could only dream of.

"They're testing our strength!" she shouted over the clash of steel. "Probing for weaknesses!"

Arin blocked an imperial sword with a staff, his untrained Vahl making the impact jar his bones. He was Threadwake facing Threadholders. Outmatched in every way.

Then Darro was there.

The old man moved through the battle like a ghost, his staff a blur. Where he passed, imperials fell. Not from deadly strikes, but from precise taps that disrupted their Vahl, leaving them disoriented and vulnerable.

"The half-step!" Lira breathed. "I've never seen it used in combat!"

Arin watched, mesmerized. Darro wasn't fighting. He was... correcting. Adjusting the flow of battle like a musician tuning an instrument. Each touch restored balance, however briefly.

But for every imperial Darro neutralized, two more took their place. The Solarans had numbers. And discipline.

"Fall back to the roundhouses!" Darro ordered. "Defend the children!"

The retreat became a rout. The Fajar fought bravely, but they were outnumbered and outmatched. Arin saw Kala fall, an arrow in her shoulder. Saw Seren dragged away by imperial soldiers.

"No!" he screamed, surging forward.

Lira grabbed his arm. "They want you to follow! It's a trap!"

But Arin was already moving, his Vahl responding to his rage. The Threads screamed a warning he ignored. All he saw was Seren's frightened face.

Then the world exploded.

Literally. An imperial firepot hit the elders' platform, shattering the ancient stones. The locked box flew into the air, landing near Arin's feet.

The lid had cracked open.

For a moment, everything stopped. The fighting, the shouting, even the wind seemed to hold its breath. From the cracked box poured not objects, but light. And memories.

Arin saw generations of Fajar elders staring out at this same village. Felt their hopes, their fears, their love for this patch of earth. He saw Darro as a young man, full of fire. Saw Seren's grandmother singing the dawn-song.

The memories washed over him, through him. And in their wake, he understood.

The box wasn't for reading. It was for becoming.

Darro appeared beside him, his face grim. "It's time."

He lifted the box, the remaining memories swirling around them like fireflies. Then he spoke words Arin had never heard, in a language older than the mountains.

The light intensified, wrapping around them. The imperial soldiers shielded their eyes, stumbling back.

When the light faded, Arin and Darro were gone.

The locked box lay empty on the scorched earth. The Dawn had died.

And from its ashes, something new would have to rise.
