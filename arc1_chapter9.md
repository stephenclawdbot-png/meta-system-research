# ASHES OF THE DAWN
## Chapter 9: The Testing Ground

The war-drums beat through the night, a relentless rhythm that stripped away sleep. By dawn, the Keth camp was alive with purposeful motion. Warriors checked gear, sharpened blades, moved with the grim efficiency of people expecting battle.

Torvin summoned them as the sun cleared the eastern peaks. He stood before a practice ground marked by straw dummies and weapon racks, his massive arms crossed.

"Your people need to earn their keep," he said without preamble. "Show me what Fajar warriors can do."

The Fajar looked to Darro, but it was Arin who stepped forward. "We're not warriors. We're shamans."

"Shamans die just as easily," Torvin said. "Better to learn to fight than to rely on tricks."

"The Hollow isn't a trick," Arin said.

"Prove it."

Torvin gestured to a Keth warrior standing nearby. The man was younger than Torvin but built with the same solid strength. His Vahl was a tightly coiled spring.

"This is Roric," Torvin said. "One of our best. Fight him."

"Fight?" Arin repeated, stunned.

"Spar," Torvin clarified. "Show me what your... Hollow... can do against steel."

Darro moved to intervene, but Lira caught his arm. "Let him," she whispered. "This is the test."

Arin looked at Roric. The Keth warrior watched him with calm confidence. This was routine for him. Just another day in the war-camp.

"I don't want to hurt anyone," Arin said.

Roric smiled, a quick flash of teeth. "You won't."

They faced each other on the practice ground. The Keth gathered around, their expressions ranging from curiosity to outright skepticism.

Arin reached for the Threads, but they felt distant. Muted by the Hollow's lingering echo. He couldn't summon the focused Vahl he'd need for combat.

Roric moved first, a testing lunge that was more feint than attack. Arin dodged clumsily, his movements those of a scholar, not a fighter.

Laughter rippled through the watching Keth.

"Use your power," Torvin commanded.

Arin closed his eyes, reaching for the Hollow. But this time, it resisted him. The space between spaces felt slippery, unwilling to be commanded.

Roric attacked in earnest now, his movements fluid and precise. Arin dodged and stumbled, his lack of training painfully obvious.

"Stop!" Darro's voice cut through the noise.

The practice ground fell silent. Darro stood at the edge of the circle, his face dark with anger.

"This is not a game," he said to Torvin. "The boy is exhausted. His connection to the Vahl is damaged."

"Damaged how?" Torvin asked.

"The Hollow takes," Darro said. "It doesn't give back what it takes."

Arin felt the truth of it. The emptiness where his Vahl should be. The cold that had settled in his bones.

"Then he's useless to us," Torvin said.

"No," Lira countered. "He's different. Not useless."

Roric lowered his practice sword. "The boy has heart. But heart doesn't win battles."

Arin looked at the faces around him. The skeptical Keth. The worried Fajar. Darro's disappointed expression.

He remembered Amon's journal. The warning about trying to control the Hollow. About unbecoming.

Maybe he'd been approaching this wrong.

"I don't need to fight him," Arin said quietly.

All eyes turned to him.

"What?" Torvin asked.

"The Hollow isn't for fighting," Arin said. "It's for... being. Differently."

He walked to the center of the practice ground and sat cross-legged in the dust. Closed his eyes. Stopped reaching for anything.

Just... let go.

The Hollow flooded in. Not as power, but as absence. The world faded around him. Sounds became distant echoes. The watching Keth became faint shadows.

Roric took a step toward him, then stopped. His face showed confusion. "I... can't see him properly."

"What do you mean?" Torvin demanded.

"It's like he's not there," Roric said. "But I know he is."

Arin remained in the Hollow, not trying to control it. Just existing within it. He felt the Threads around him - Roric's aggressive Vahl, the Keth warriors' disciplined energy, Darro's ancient power.

But they couldn't touch him here. In the Hollow, he was untouchable.

After a long moment, he let the Hollow recede. The world snapped back into focus.

Roric was staring at him with something like awe. "How did you do that?"

"I didn't," Arin said. "I stopped trying."

Torvin studied him with new respect. "That could be useful. Against imperial scouts."

"It has uses," Darro admitted grudgingly. "But costs."

Lira helped Arin to his feet. "You all right?"

He nodded, though he felt drained. The Hollow had taken more than it gave. Again.

"We'll continue your training," Torvin decided. "But differently. Lira, work with him. Teach him to move like a warrior."

Lira nodded. "He learns fast."

As the Keth dispersed, Darro pulled Arin aside. "Be careful, boy. Power always has a price."

"I know," Arin said.

But as he looked at the war-camp, at the Keth preparing for a war they might not win, he wondered if the price of having no power was even higher.

The testing was over. The real work was about to begin.
